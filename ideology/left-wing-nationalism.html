📣Left-Wing Nationalism

Patriotic Worker Power Centre

Unite the nation to throw off foreign domination, protect national resources, and use state power to lift up working people.

In simple words

Imagine an oil field or coal mine in your home district that has been owned for decades by a foreign corporation. All the profits are sent to a bank account overseas, while the local workers earn low wages and the surrounding roads and schools lie broken. A left-wing nationalist says: that wealth belongs to our nation. Take control of our natural resources, invest the money in our own hospitals and schools, and give ordinary citizens pride and dignity in their own land.

Left-wing nationalism brings together two powerful ideas that people often think are opposites: deep love for one's country and socialist economic policies. Unlike right-wing nationalists, who often define the nation by race, religion or inherited social hierarchy, left-wing nationalists define the nation by its working people. They view national sovereignty not as an excuse to oppress minorities, but as an essential shield against imperialist exploitation and global corporate greed.

In India, the 1969 nationalisation of major private banks forced finance out of elite urban circles and sent thousands of new bank branches into rural villages, providing credit to small farmers and entrepreneurs. In Egypt, Gamal Abdel Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal and redistributed land to peasant farmers. In Mexico, President Lázaro Cárdenas nationalised foreign-owned oil fields in 1938, creating the state petroleum firm Pemex and establishing national control over crucial energy wealth.

This philosophy was central to anti-colonial independence struggles across Asia, Africa and Latin America. It argues that a country cannot be truly free politically until its working people own and control their own economic destiny.

Core beliefs

  • 📣Domestic self-reliance: Protecting local jobs and natural resources from foreign exploitation.
  • 🏭Pro-worker state: Government invests heavily in national industries and workers wages.
  • 🛡️Universal public services: State-funded free healthcare, education, and pensions for all citizens.

When it worked

Inspiring example

Gamal Abdel Nasser's reforms

Egypt · 1954–1970

Nationalised the foreign-controlled Suez Canal, reclaimed land for poor peasant farmers and built the massive Aswan High Dam, securing electricity, flood control and irrigation for millions while restoring Arab pride.

Inspiring example

Bank nationalisation in India

India · 1969

The government nationalised 14 major private commercial banks, rapidly spreading bank branches into unserved rural villages and directing essential credit to small farmers, artisans and small enterprises.

Inspiring example

Mexico's oil nationalisation

Mexico · 1938

President Lázaro Cárdenas expropriated foreign-owned oil assets and established the national oil company Pemex, uniting the Mexican public and ensuring petroleum revenues funded national education and public infrastructure.

When it went wrong

Cautionary example

Bhutto's nationalisations

Pakistan · 1972–1977

In the name of the nation and the poor, the government took over banks, heavy industry and even flour and rice mills; private investment collapsed and growth slowed.

Cautionary example

Gaddafi's dictatorship in Libya

Libya · 1969–2011

Oil wealth was used for popular welfare programs, but the regime banned private retail shops, outlawed independent political debate, spent billions funding foreign militant groups and executed political opponents.

What supporters say

  • Securing sovereign national control over natural resources, ports and finance shields developing countries from foreign economic coercion and unfair trade terms.
  • It unites citizens across religious and regional lines around a shared patriotic project of building schools, healthcare and home-grown industry.
  • Anti-colonial patriotism restores the dignity and self-respect of nations whose culture and economy were crushed by colonial rule.

What critics say

  • Overzealous nationalisation often ruins productive businesses, scares away essential foreign technology and capital, and leads to shortages and inflation.
  • State-run industries frequently fall victim to political corruption, gross mismanagement and patronage jobs for party loyalists.
  • Nationalist rhetoric, even when wrapped in socialist language, can easily be weaponised against domestic critics and labeled as treason.

Famous figures

  • Bhagat Singh

    India · 1907–1931

    Revolutionary freedom fighter who combined uncompromising patriotic struggle against British colonial rule with Marxist socialism, declaring that independence must abolish the exploitation of man by man.

  • Subhas Chandra Bose

    India · 1897–1945

    Charismatic leader who formed the Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj) to wage armed struggle against the British Empire, advocating a planned socialist economy and total national unity across all faiths.

  • Gamal Abdel Nasser

    Egypt · 1918–1970

    President of Egypt who championed Arab socialism, anti-imperialist non-alignment and national pride, nationalising the Suez Canal and becoming a hero of decolonisation across the Global South.

Common questions

Can nationalism genuinely be left-wing?

Yes. While nationalism in Europe has often been associated with right-wing, exclusionary movements, nationalism in formerly colonised nations was predominantly left-wing. In Asia, Africa and Latin America, patriotic movements fought to liberate their people from foreign empires and corporate monopolies. For these movements, patriotism was about national self-determination, collective sovereignty and social justice for all citizens, rather than racial or religious chauvinism.

Was Bhagat Singh a socialist or a nationalist?

He was both. Bhagat Singh began his political journey as an ardent young patriot fighting for Indian liberation. As he read extensively in prison and organised the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA), he made it clear that replacing British rulers with wealthy Indian landlords and capitalists would not bring true freedom. For him, genuine independence required overthrowing imperialism and establishing a socialist republic that served peasants and workers.

What is the key difference between left-wing and right-wing nationalism?

Right-wing nationalism usually defines national identity through shared ancestry, ancient religious traditions or cultural uniformity, often viewing ethnic or religious minorities with suspicion while supporting private business. Left-wing nationalism, by contrast, defines the nation by its common working citizens regardless of caste or creed, prioritises sovereign public control over key economic sectors, and directs its hostility outward against foreign exploitation and imperial domination.
